# References

1. CDC. *Overview and Methods for the Youth Risk Behavior Surveillance System — United States, 2023.* MMWR Supplement, 73(4), 2024.
   https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/73/su/su7304a1.htm
   — Official methodology for the dataset itself: sampling design, survey weights, and how questions are administered. Cite this to justify your data source and any weighting decisions.

2. CDC. *Youth Risk Behavior Survey Data Summary & Trends Report: 2013–2023.*
   https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/252282
   — National prevalence trends for tobacco use and other risk behaviours across a decade of survey cycles. Use this to sanity-check your own prevalence numbers and to cite baseline trend figures in your intro.

3. Wellman, R.J., et al. *Predictors of the Onset of Cigarette Smoking: A Systematic Review of Longitudinal Population-Based Studies in Youth.* American Journal of Preventive Medicine, 2016.
   https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27180028/
   — A systematic review of what actually predicts youth smoking onset (peer influence, parental smoking, impulsivity, academic performance, etc.). This is your strongest source for justifying *which* variables to include as predictors in your logistic regression / random forest.

4. CDC. *Tobacco Product Use Among Middle and High School Students — National Youth Tobacco Survey, United States, 2024.* MMWR, 73(41), 2024.
   https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/73/wr/mm7341a2.htm
   — A complementary national survey (NYTS, not YRBSS) on youth tobacco use — useful for cross-checking whether your YRBSS findings align with a separate, independent data source.

5. *(Optional 5th)* Audrain-McGovern, J., et al. *Child and adolescent predictors of smoking involvement in emerging adulthood.* PMC, 2019.
   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6552681/
   — Longitudinal look at which childhood/adolescent factors predict smoking later on; useful if you want to discuss long-term risk trajectories in your limitations/discussion section.
